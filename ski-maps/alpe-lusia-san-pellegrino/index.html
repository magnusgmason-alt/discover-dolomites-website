Alpe Lusia · San Pellegrino

Moena, Alpe Lusia, Passo San Pellegrino and Falcade

Two linked mountains on the border of Trentino and the Veneto: family-friendly Alpe Lusia above Moena, and the high pass of San Pellegrino, where World Cup racers will drop down La VolatA this January.

South of Moena, the road to Passo San Pellegrino climbs out of Val di Fassa to a broad saddle at 1,918 m, then drops east to Falcade in the Veneto. Around it lies one of the most varied corners of Dolomiti Superski: about 95 km of pistes in two areas, by Visit Trentino's count, Alpe Lusia and San Pellegrino–Falcade, sold together as the Tre Valli, the Three Valleys.

This is a place for locals and families as much as for visitors: fewer international crowds, plenty of snow-sure terrain, and in January 2027 the women's World Cup speed circuit.

The Three Valleys

Two Mountains, One Pass

The two halves are not linked by piste. Alpe Lusia rises between Moena in Val di Fassa and Bellamonte on the Fiemme side; San Pellegrino–Falcade spreads across both flanks of the pass, about ten minutes' drive from Moena. A ski bus connects them, and a single area pass covers both, as does the Dolomiti Superski pass (prices in ski passes and prices).

Together they offer 70 runs: 32 blue, 30 red and 8 black, served by 23 lifts. Expect sweeping views across the Catinaccio (Rosengarten), Sassolungo, Latemar and the Pale di San Martino from the high points.

Alpe Lusia

Moena's Home Mountain

Lusia is the smaller area, with about 25 km of pistes, 24 runs and eight lifts: four gondolas, three chairs and a single surface lift. From the base at Ronchi (I Ronc), near Moena, the gondola stops at Valbona, which has a ski school, a children's mini-club, a playground and a hut with a sun terrace for non-skiers.

The Moena side holds the red and black runs, led by Piavac, the area's most challenging piste; Canalone Tomba and Fiamme Oro 1 are also graded black. Above, from Le Cune, you can descend towards Campo and follow a smooth ski-way through the Paneveggio Nature Park. Families gravitate to the wide, sunny, easy runs of Castelir, while the slopes towards Bellamonte hold the Lusia Boardercross and Morea Snowpark (see snowboarding and snowparks).

The Pass

San Pellegrino and Col Margherita

The larger area straddles the pass, with more than 60 km of pistes and 15 lifts between roughly 1,150 m and 2,514 m. Around the pass, families and beginners have dedicated zones and easy blues, while the Col Margherita cable car climbs to 2,514 m, the top of the area.

From the summit you can ski back to the pass or follow the Tre Valli Dolomiti ski tour east past Lago di Cavia and Le Buse to Falcade. The black Col Margherita piste runs down towards Falcade; Nuova Cima Uomo is the area's third black.

San Pellegrino's racing piste is graded black and steep enough to stage elite competition. What'S New

Winter 2026/27

The news this season is less about lifts than infrastructure: renewed snowmaking on the pistes of both Alpe Lusia and San Pellegrino, the expanded LaVolatA finish area, and a new bar-restaurant at the base station of the Col Margherita cable car.

The area also stages sociable events such as Trentino Ski Sunrise and Bubbles at Sunset on Col Margherita; dates are announced each season. Cross-country skiers will find 37 km of tracks at the pass (see beyond skiing).

Ski the pass in the morning sun, then follow the Tre Valli tour down to Falcade for lunch.

Who It Suits

Who Should Come

Families and intermediates get the most from the Three Valleys: Valbona, Castelir and the easy runs at the pass are fine places to learn (see skiing with kids). Strong skiers should not dismiss it: La VolatA, Col Margherita and Piavac are serious descents, and three snowparks and several boardercross lines keep riders busy.

On the Veneto side of the pass, Falcade is where the Tre Valli tour ends. A ski bus runs from the village to the lifts at the valley station, and its own funpark sits on the slopes above.

Local tip

Planning the Three Valleys

  • Start early at the pass when the north-side blues are freshly groomed.
  • Allow time for the bus between Lusia and San Pellegrino; there is no piste link.
  • Ski Col Margherita in the afternoon if you are ending your day in Falcade.
