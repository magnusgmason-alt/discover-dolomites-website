Cortina d’Ampezzo

Tofane, Cinque Torri, Lagazuoi and Faloria-Cristallo

Twice an Olympic host, Cortina pairs a stylish town with three very different ski mountains, the legendary Olympia delle Tofane and a cable-car ride to the old front line on Lagazuoi.

Cortina d'Ampezzo sits at 1,224 m in a wide bowl of the Valle d'Ampezzo, the Veneto's great mountain resort, with the Tofane on one side and Faloria and Cristallo on the other. It has been a fashion trendsetter since the 1950s, and its main shopping street, Corso Italia, is still the place to see and be seen after the lifts close.

The 1956 Winter Olympics made its name: 32 nations and 923 athletes came, and Italy broadcast the Games live on television for the first time. Seventy years later, in February 2026, the women's alpine races of Milano Cortina were held here on the Olympia delle Tofane.

The Lie Of The Land

Three Mountains, One Valley

Cortina's skiing is not one interlinked circus but three distinct areas spread around the valley: the Tofane above the west side of town, Faloria-Cristallo to the east, and Cinque Torri-Lagazuoi out towards Passo Falzarego. Together they count 66 slopes and 26 lifts.

The local Valle pass and the Dolomiti Superski pass (see ski passes and prices) add San Vito di Cadore and Auronzo-Misurina, for 120 km in all. Plan each day around one area and use the ski buses to switch sides (see getting around).

Cortina's largest area spreads across the lower slopes at Socrepes, Pocol, Rumerlo, Pié Tofana and Pomedes, where most of its 20 blue runs make easy cruising. Above, the Freccia nel Cielo cable car climbs via Col Druscié to Ra Valles, a high bowl skied on natural snow only, and on to the 3,244 m summit of Tofana di Mezzo. The big names are here too: Olympia delle Tofane, Vertigine and Col Druscié A (see famous runs).

Tip Ra Valles has no snowmaking, so it is at its best after a good fall of natural snow.

Rifugio Faloria
2,123 m, at the top of the cable car

The Faloria cable car leaves from the town centre, so many guests can walk to the lift. At the top, Rifugio Faloria has a big sun terrace over the valley. The area is compact and technical: Stratondi, Scoiattolo, Canalone Franchetti and Vitelli, which hosted the 1956 Olympic giant slalom. The Cristallo chairlifts start at Rio Gere, about 8 km from the centre on the road to Passo Tre Croci.

Tip Faloria can stay open late into spring: in 2026 its cable car was scheduled to run until 3 May.

Towers And Tunnels

Cinque Torri and Lagazuoi

The third area, 5 Torri-Lagazuoi, has 16 slopes (11 red, 5 blue) and 8 lifts across Cinque Torri, Averau-Giau, Col Gallina and Lagazuoi. It is the most scenic corner of Cortina, and the one with the most history: the rock towers and the Lagazuoi tunnels were part of the Great War front, today an open-air museum (see the Great War ski tour).

Armentarola run

A cable car swings up from the pass to Rifugio Lagazuoi. From the top, the long Armentarola run leads through the Hidden Valley into Alta Badia, ending with a tow behind sled horses: the classic way to ski between the two resorts (see the Great War ski tour).

Tip The Great War Tour also passes here, so ride up early on fine days.

The Cortina Skyline gondola links the Pocol area with Cinque Torri in about 15 minutes, between car parks at Son dei Prade and Bai de Dones, so Tofane skiers can carry on towards Falzarego without a bus.

Milano Cortina 2026

The Olympic Venues

In February 2026 the Tofane Alpine Skiing Centre staged every women's Olympic alpine race, from the downhill on 8 February to the slalom on 18 February, then the Paralympic alpine events in March. The downhill started at Rifugio Pomedes and the super-G at Rifugio Duca d'Aosta, with both finishes a short walk from the town.

The course is the Olympia delle Tofane, opened for the 1956 Games: a 2,560 m race course by the FIS's measure (about 2.4 km and 760 m vertical as a public piste, says Cortina's tourist board), with the famous Schuss reaching a 65% gradient (more in famous runs). In town, the Cortina Sliding Centre was rebuilt on the site of the historic Eugenio Monti track, used from 1923 to 2008, for bobsleigh, skeleton and luge. The Olympic Ice Stadium, built for 1956, hosted curling, wheelchair curling and the Paralympic closing ceremony.

Few resorts can say they hosted the Winter Games twice, seventy years apart, on the same mountain.

Did you know?

The gondola that missed the Games

A new Apollonio–Socrepes gondola, with three stations and 10-person cabins for up to 2,400 people per hour, was built to link the town with the Olympia slope. Built for about €35 million on landslide-prone ground, it missed the Olympics; in late March 2026 it still awaited safety tests. Check whether it is running before you plan around it.

Who It Suits

Is Cortina for You?

Cortina suits skiers who want a real town as well as a ski area. Intermediates have the Tofane blues and the reds of Cinque Torri; experts head for the Faloria-Cristallo blacks and the Olympic slopes. Because the areas are separate, it rewards a car or a well-timed bus more than a ski-in, ski-out stay (see where to stay).

Local tip

  • Pick a side each day: Tofane and Cinque Torri are linked by the Skyline gondola; Faloria-Cristallo is a separate day out.
  • Helmets: compulsory for all ages on Italian pistes (see safety and slope rules).
