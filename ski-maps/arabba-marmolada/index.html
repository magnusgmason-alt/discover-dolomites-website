Arabba · Marmolada

Porta Vescovo, Passo Pordoi and the Marmolada glacier

A small Ladin village with big, north-facing slopes, and beside it the Marmolada, the highest mountain in the Dolomites, with a cable car to 3,265 m and a 12 km run back down.

Arabba is tiny: a cluster of hotels and houses at 1,602 m in the Fodom valley, a Ladin community in the Veneto. Yet it sits at a crossroads of the Sella Ronda, between Passo Campolongo and Passo Pordoi, and its lifts climb straight from the village onto some of the most serious pistes in the Dolomiti Superski network.

Across the valley stands the Marmolada, the 'Queen of the Dolomites', its glacier rising to 3,343 m. From Malga Ciapela a cable car lifts skiers to Punta Rocca in about 12 minutes, and La Bellunese carries them back down over roughly 1,800 m of vertical. Together, Arabba and the Marmolada form one compact, high and snow-sure ski area.

The Area

Small Village, Steep Ground

The Arabba–Marmolada area is modest in size, about 63 km of slopes by the local tourist board's count, but it covers an exceptional range of height: from Malga Ciapela at 1,450 m to Punta Rocca at 3,265 m. Dolomiti Superski lists 25 lifts, 20 around Arabba and five on the Marmolada. The local tourist board promises everything from easy carving slopes to demanding black runs, and the altitude keeps the snow in good shape late into spring.

The main village of the Fodom valley sits at the foot of the Sella massif, with lifts a few metres from the hotels on both sides of the road. On one side the Burz chairlift climbs to a viewpoint over the village; on the other, the cable car rises to Porta Vescovo. Arabba is a starting point for both the Sella Ronda and the Great War ski tour.

Tip Pick a hotel by which side of the village you want to ski first; both lift bases are in the centre.

The ridge above Arabba is the area's high point and its viewing platform: the Marmolada glacier fills the southern skyline, with Piz Boè, the Sassolungo and the Catinaccio groups behind. Arabba's demanding black runs are described in our guide to famous runs.

On the other side of the village, the Burz chairlift (1,936 m) leads on to Bec de Roces (2,160 m), a 360° panorama on the Sella Ronda, and towards Cherz and Pralongià, the back door into Alta Badia (see Alta Badia). Burz is the spot for the enrosadira, the pink glow on the peaks in late afternoon.

Lifts Worth Knowing

Links in Every Direction

  • Porta Vescovo Funifor: the cable car from Arabba to the ridge.
  • Europa gondola: the first leg from Arabba towards Passo Padon and the Marmolada.
  • Portados gondola, Carpazza and Alpenrose chairlifts: the climb to Passo Pordoi (2,239 m), on the Sella Ronda towards Val di Fassa (see Val di Fassa).
  • Fodom gondola: links Passo Pordoi with the Arabba side; from the pass, a cable car rises to Sass Pordoi at 2,950 m (winter service from 22 December in 2026/27, per Val di Fassa).
  • Burz and Le Pale chairlifts: the gentler, sunny side towards Bec de Roces and Alta Badia.
  • Padon and Antercrëp six-seat chairlifts: the Marmolada sector, below Passo Padon (2,369 m).

The cable car climbs in stages from Malga Ciapela via Serauta, where there is a self-service restaurant, to Punta Rocca, the highest point you can reach by lift in the Dolomiti Superski area. On clear days the view stretches to the Venetian lagoon. From Arabba you reach it on skis via the Europa gondola, two chairlifts and Passo Padon.

Tip Check that the cable car is running before committing: in 2025/26 it opened on 13 December, later than the rest of the area.

La Bellunese begins at Punta Rocca with wide, calm slopes on the glacier, then becomes more varied lower down as it swings past Passo Fedaia to Malga Ciapela, about 12 km and some 1,800 m of descent in all. Its gradient is intermediate, but it is long, high and cold, so ski it early. Its profile is in our guide to famous runs.

Did you know?

A city in the ice

During the First World War, Austrian engineer Leo Handl dug a network of tunnels inside the Marmolada glacier, the 'Eisstadt' or city of ice. Planned at 8 km, it grew to about 12 km by spring 1917. The front line and its relics are the subject of our guide to the Great War ski tour.

Who It Suits

For Skiers Who Want More

Arabba suits strong intermediates and experts best, along with anyone planning to ski the Sella Ronda and the Marmolada in the same week. Beginners are better served next door in Alta Badia (see Alta Badia). Late-season skiers gain most: with planned closing on 18 April 2027, three weeks after Easter (28 March), Arabba runs longest of the Sella Ronda areas.

Local tip

  • Do the Marmolada on a clear, calm day; on the glacier the wind and cold bite hard (cold-weather advice: our guide to safety and slope rules).
  • Start early from Arabba: Europa gondola, Passo Padon, then the cable car, and La Bellunese before the afternoon sun softens the lower slopes.
  • Book the Superpremière deal (until 19 Dec 2026) or late-season Springdays for good-value weeks (see ski passes and prices).
