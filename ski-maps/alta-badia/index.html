Alta Badia

Corvara, Colfosco, La Villa and San Cassiano

145 kmRuns on this map
44Lifts
5Toboggan runs
1Superski pass

Wide, sunny pistes, Ladin villages and some of the Dolomites' finest mountain food, with the Gran Risa World Cup slope for those who want a fight. More than half of Alta Badia is rated easy.

Alta Badia is the upper Val Badia, a Ladin-speaking valley where the Sella massif, the Sassongher and the pale walls of the Fanes-Sennes group close the horizon. Its ski area rolls across broad, sun-filled bowls between Corvara, Colfosco, La Villa, San Cassiano, Badia and La Val, and for once the statistics favour the cautious: of 130 km of slopes, 74 km are graded blue.

This is the Dolomites' most relaxed big ski area, yet it has teeth. The Gran Risa above La Villa hosts the men's World Cup giant slalom each December, and Corvara sits on the Sella Ronda, with Arabba and Val Gardena a lift ride away.

Best forBeginners and intermediates, families, and anyone who loves long cruising runs and great food.

The Area

Built for Cruising

The heart of Alta Badia is a high, rolling plateau around Pralongià, Piz Sorega and Piz La Ila, linked by chairlifts and wide pistes that drain down to the villages on every side. The valley's own figures give 130 km of slopes: 74 km blue, 47 km red and just 9 km black, served by 54 lifts. More than 90 piste machines groom the runs every night.

Pralongià

Conturines, Sassongher, Marmolada

The high meadow at the centre of the network, where runs from Corvara, La Villa and San Cassiano meet. From here chairlifts and pistes continue over Cherz to Arabba, a second link between the two valleys alongside the Sella Ronda route over Passo Campolongo. On clear days the view takes in the Fodom and Badia valleys and the Marmolada glacier.

Tip A natural lunch stop on a day between Alta Badia and Arabba; huts are in our guide to mountain huts and food.

The busiest village and the obvious base, at the foot of the Sassongher. The Col Alto gondola rises straight from the centre to the Pralongià plateau, while the Boè gondola climbs towards the Sella, where the Vallon chairlift serves the Vallon-Boè black, the area's high, steep corner below the Piz Boè (3,152 m), the only three-thousander in the Sella group.

Best Runs

From Gran Risa to La Crusc

The Gran Risa is the valley's showpiece: 1,255 m long, with 448 m of vertical, an average gradient of 36% and a maximum of 69%. World Cup giant slaloms have been held here since 1985, and Alberto Tomba won four races on it. On 20 December 2026 the men race their giant slalom; on 21 December the organisers add a night slalom, the first in the slope's history.

Above Badia (Pedraces), the La Crusc lifts climb towards the Santa Croce sanctuary beneath the cliffs of the Sas dla Crusc. La Crusc 1 is a blue of 2.5 km and 500 m of vertical, a rare chance for intermediates to ski a long descent below a Dolomite wall. The Vallon-Boè black and the long Sella Ronda links are described in famous runs and the Sella Ronda.

Lifts Worth Knowing

New and Notable

  • Col Alto gondola (Corvara): the quickest way from the village to the plateau.
  • Boè gondola and Vallon chairlift: the high route towards the Sella and the black runs.
  • Pralongià six-seat chairlift: the crossroads of the plateau.
  • Costoratta: an eight-seat chairlift with weather hood.
  • Falzarego–Lagazuoi cable car: listed with Alta Badia's lifts, the start of the Hidden Valley (see the Great War ski tour).

For 2026/27 Dolomiti Superski lists a new slope, 5c, in the Arlara area, several older lifts replaced by modern equivalents of the same type, and new photovoltaic panels on lift stations.

Who It Suits

Gentle by Design

Alta Badia is ideal for beginners, families and intermediates who prefer long cruises to moguls, and for anyone who skis partly for lunch: the valley's huts and restaurants are covered in our guide to mountain huts and food. Experts will find enough with the Gran Risa, the Vallon-Boè black and day trips to Arabba (see Arabba and the Marmolada), but may want more steeps over a full week.

Local tip

  • Base yourself in Corvara for the Sella Ronda in either direction, or in La Villa to be in the middle of the valley.
  • Around the World Cup on 20–21 December 2026, expect the Gran Risa to be busy or closed for racing; check live slope status first.
  • Alta Badia's four ski kindergartens and 350-plus instructors make it easy to split a family for the morning (more in ski schools).
