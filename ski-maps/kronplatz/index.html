Kronplatz · Plan de Corones

One mountain, lifts on every side

84 kmRuns on this map
23Lifts
15Toboggan runs
1Superski pass

A rounded summit wired with gondolas from every side, five serious black runs, museums on the top and three brand-new lifts for 2026/27: no wonder Kronplatz bills itself as South Tyrol's leading ski area.

Kronplatz (Plan de Corones) rises to 2,275 m above the Puster Valley (Val Pusteria), between Brunico (Bruneck), Valdaora (Olang) and San Vigilio di Marebbe (St. Vigil in Enneberg). Unlike the jagged peaks around it, it is a broad dome, and lifts climb it from all directions, so the whole ski area spills down its flanks like the spokes of a wheel.

That shape makes Kronplatz unusually easy to read. Almost every run ends at a valley station or a lift back to the summit, and 21 of its lifts are gondolas, a blessing on a cold January morning.

The Mountain

A Dome Wired for Skiing

The Kronplatz ski area counts 125 km of slopes on 51 runs: 26 easy, 16 medium and 9 difficult. Its 32 lifts include 21 gondolas, five chairlifts and six drag lifts, plus ski buses and a train that are part of the network. The biggest single climb is the Kronplatz 2000 gondola, with 1,314 m of vertical.

The summit is a destination in itself. The Concordia 2000 peace bell rings out across the plateau; beside it stand the Messner Mountain Museum Corones, designed by Zaha Hadid and devoted to mountaineering, and Lumen, a museum of mountain photography. Both make good refuges on a stormy day (see beyond skiing).

New For 2026/27

Three Lifts, Three Slopes

Kronplatz is one of the big winners of Dolomiti Superski's investment for 2026/27. On the Brunico side, the new Kronplatz 1+2 gondola runs from Riscone (Reischach) to the summit with 10-seat cabins and a relocated mid-station. On the San Vigilio side, the Ruis gondola replaces the old cabin lift from the Furkel pass area, and at Valdaora a completely new Dolomiti chairlift has covered, heated seats.

Three slopes come with them. Lumen is a red run of 2.7 km and 775 m vertical, extended down to the new Kronplatz 1+2 mid-station; the short blue Weiden (650 m) links the old mid-station with it; and the blue Dolomiti (about 1.5 km, 333 m vertical) runs alongside the new chairlift.

Brunico is the most practical base if you want a real town, with shops and restaurants, after skiing. Riscone, just above it, is the main gateway, now with the 10-seat gondola straight to the top. Rail travellers can arrive at the valley station at Ried, near Percha, without a car (see getting there).

Tip The new gondola carries skis inside the cabin, so keep them with you.

Valdaora's lifts climb the eastern flank, where the new Dolomiti chairlift and its blue run add a fresh sector. The heated, covered seats and conveyor-belt loading make it easy for children and beginners to ride.

The southern side looks towards the Dolomites proper. From here the Piculin run and a ski bus between Piculin, Sompunt and Sponata connect Kronplatz with Alta Badia and the Sella Ronda, so a day can start on Kronplatz and end on the Sella circuit (see the Sella Ronda and Alta Badia).

Tip Check the last ski-bus times back from Alta Badia before you set off.

For Experts

The Black Five

Kronplatz sells its steep side as the Black Five: Sylvester, Herrnegg, Pre da Peres, Erta and Piculin. Erta hosts a FIS women's giant slalom. Official sources disagree on exact lengths, but all agree that Sylvester and Herrnegg are the long ones and Pre da Peres the shortest; ski them early, while the snow is firm and the pistes are quiet.

On Kronplatz the question is never how to get back up, only which side of the mountain to ski down.

Who It Suits

Is Kronplatz for You?

With half its runs graded easy and gondolas almost everywhere, Kronplatz suits families, beginners progressing to blues and anyone who dislikes drag lifts. Snowboarders have the Snowpark Kronplatz and the Jib Park Korer (see snowboarding and snowparks). Experts get the Black Five. What it lacks is the rock scenery underfoot: the views are of the Dolomites rather than among them.

Did you know?

An Olympic neighbour

The Kronplatz holiday region includes the Antholz valley, whose Anterselva Biathlon Arena at 1,600 m held every biathlon race of the 2026 Winter Olympics, with room for 19,000 spectators, the largest venue of the Games. It has hosted international races since 1971.

Local tip

  • Take the train: the Ski Pustertal Express links Kronplatz and 3 Zinnen Dolomites in about 35 minutes, and is covered by the Südtirol Guest Pass (see 3 Zinnen Dolomites).
  • Kronplatz-only pass: the local pass has slightly different high-season dates from Dolomiti Superski; high season ends on 13 March 2027 (see ski passes and prices).
  • Bad weather: the summit museums are an easy plan B.
