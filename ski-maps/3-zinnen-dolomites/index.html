3 Zinnen Dolomites

Monte Elmo, Croda Rossa and Sesto

Five linked ski mountains on the Austrian border, a 35 km circuit across them, a family hill in the middle of town and a new 10-seat gondola: the Tre Cime's home ski area is bigger than its reputation.

At the eastern end of the Puster Valley, close to the Austrian border, the villages of Sesto (Sexten), San Candido (Innichen) and Dobbiaco (Toblach) share a ski area named after the most famous peaks in the Dolomites, the Tre Cime (Drei Zinnen). The area is known as 3 Zinnen Dolomites.

It feels quieter and more rural than the Sella valleys, yet the numbers are serious: 115 km of slopes and 30 lifts, five ski mountains linked into one network, and a valley railway that brings Kronplatz within about 35 minutes.

The Layout

Five Mountains, One Network

The heart of the area is a chain of linked mountains above Sesto and Versciaco (Vierschach): Monte Elmo (Helm), Stiergarten and Croda Rossa (Rotwand), with Passo Monte Croce (Kreuzbergpass) to the south-east and the Val Comelico ski area beyond it. Around them sit smaller valley areas: Monte Baranci above San Candido, and beginner and racing slopes at Waldheim, Braies (Prags) and Rienza (Rienz) near Dobbiaco.

The area's answer to the Sella Ronda links all five mountains in one day. Some sections are joined by the SALTO ski shuttle, including the hop to the Val Comelico ski area. Guided tours are available. The resort itself warns that some trail designs may challenge snowboarders, so it is best done on skis.

Tip Start early and check the shuttle times before committing to the Comelico leg.

New For 2026/27

Helmissimo

The biggest change this winter is at Versciaco, where the Helmissimo gondola replaces the old lift up Monte Elmo. Leitner's 97 panoramic cabins seat ten each, and the ride is quicker and roomier than before. The valley run beneath it is being widened, re-routed and renamed Helmissimo, with upgraded snowmaking.

Monte Elmo is the hub, reached from both sides of the ridge. The old top station of its cable car has been turned into the Reinhold Messner Haus, a centre for sustainability, art and culture telling the mountaineer's life story, with its own cinema; it opens daily 10:00–16:00. A red run leads across to Stiergarten.

Tip The Helmjet Sexten is the quickest way up from Sesto.

The Croda Rossa side holds the area's best-known challenge, the Holzriese, two runs graded difficult (more in famous runs). Easy runs such as Bad Moos and Rotwandwiesen give mixed groups something to share.

Right in the centre of San Candido, a chairlift climbs to Rifugio Gigante Baranci. It is the area's family hill, with wide slopes, a children's zone and a 2.1 km toboggan run from the top station. On Tuesday and Friday evenings its floodlit slope opens for night skiing and night tobogganing (see beyond skiing).

Tip In 2026/27 night skiing runs from 29 December to 12 March.

The valley lifts fill in the gaps. Passo Monte Croce has extra-wide red slopes, one FIS-homologated, where Marc Girardelli trained; Waldheim in Sesto is where ski schools teach first turns; Braies is a small area beneath the north faces of the Dürrenstein; and Rienza pairs beginner slopes with the more demanding 3 Zinnen Racing Arena. Booked training slopes serve clubs and national teams.

Who It Suits

Is 3 Zinnen for You?

3 Zinnen suits families, intermediates and skiers who prefer villages with their own life to big resort centres. Experts have the Holzriese and the steep runs down to Versciaco. Snowboarders should note that more than a dozen of the 30 lifts are drag lifts (see snowboarding and snowparks).

A train to the next ski area, a gondola to the ridge, and the Tre Cime never far away.

Good to know

Combine with Kronplatz

  • Ski Pustertal Express: trains link 3 Zinnen Dolomites and Kronplatz in about 35 minutes; both ski areas are a few steps from their stations.
  • Cost: the train is covered by the Südtirol Guest Pass in winter; timetables on suedtirolmobil.info (see getting around).
  • Pass: the Dolomiti Superski pass covers both ski areas (see ski passes and prices).

Local tip

  • First lift in the Superski: Passo Monte Croce is the first lift to open in the whole Dolomiti Superski area; in 2026/27 it starts on 14 November.
  • Early skiing: on 26–31 December 2026, 2–6 January and 6–14 February 2027, the lifts on Monte Elmo, Stiergarten and Croda Rossa open at 7:30 am.
  • Ski Cross World Cup: the area hosts a stop on the circuit; dates in our guide to when to go.
