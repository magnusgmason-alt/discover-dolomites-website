Which area suits you?

Wide, gentle slopes and good ski schools.

The Alpe di Siusi is the classic first-timer's plateau: wide blue runs, easy lifts and the Sassolungo on the horizon. Alta Badia and Carezza are just as forgiving, and Plose has quiet nursery slopes above Brixen.

Short lifts, kids' parks and hotels at the piste.

By train

The Brenner line runs to Bolzano and Bressanone, and the Pusteria line on to Brunico and San Candido, right next to the Kronplatz and 3 Zinnen lifts. From Bolzano and Bressanone, buses climb to Val Gardena, Alta Badia and Val di Fassa.

By car

Winter tyres are compulsory in Italy from 15 November to 15 April, and chains may be required on passes. Driving through Austria you need a motorway vignette. Expect queues on the Brenner motorway on Saturdays.

Between valleys

Ski buses link the villages in every valley, often free with a guest card or ski pass. Once you are on the snow, the Sella Ronda lifts connect Val Gardena, Alta Badia, Arabba and Val di Fassa without a car.

Going off-piste

The Dolomites have legendary freeride lines, like the Val Mezdì couloir off the Sella. They are serious terrain.

  • Carry an avalanche transceiver, shovel and probe, and know how to use them.
  • Read the avalanche bulletin every morning before you go.
  • Hire a local mountain guide for anything beyond the piste markers.
  • Never ski off-piste alone.

08

Beyond skiing.

For rest days, mixed groups and the long winter evenings.

Mountain huts

Lunch on a sun terrace is half the point. Many huts serve South Tyrolean and Ladin dishes, and the best fill up by 12:30, so book in high season.

Toboggan runs

Most valleys have floodlit toboggan runs, and some huts open for dinner and a sledge ride back down. Helmets are required on sleds too.

Snowshoes & winter walks

Prepared winter trails cross the Alpe di Siusi, Carezza, Fanes and the 3 Zinnen area. Stick to marked routes and check the avalanche bulletin.

Cross-country & spas

Dobbiaco's Nordic Arena and the Marcialonga trails in Fiemme and Fassa are classics. Afterwards, QC Terme Dolomiti in Pozza di Fassa is the place to thaw out.

09

Safety and slope rules.

Italy has some of the strictest ski rules in the Alps. The short version:

Helmet

Compulsory for everyone

Since 2025, every skier, snowboarder and sledger of any age must wear a certified helmet (EN 1077). Fines are €100–150.

Insured

Third-party liability cover

Compulsory on the slopes. Lift offices sell a daily add-on with your pass if your own travel insurance does not cover it.

€250+

No alcohol or drugs

Skiing under the influence is banned, with fines from €250 to €1,000. Take the hut grappa after the last run.

112

Emergency number

Call 112 for mountain rescue. Most pistes are numbered, so note the number where you are.

Bulletin

Check the avalanche report

Every morning at avalanche.report for South Tyrol and Trentino, and on the ARPAV site for Veneto (Cortina, Civetta, Arabba).

No uphill

Pistes are one-way

Walking or skinning up a piste is banned except where marked. Stop at the side of the slope, never under a ridge.

Book-ahead and packing checklist.

What to sort out before you leave, and what to bring.

Book ahead

  • Accommodation6–9 months ahead for Christmas, New Year and February.
  • Ski schoolChildren's group lessons in holiday weeks fill up first.
  • Equipment hireOnline is cheaper and skips the morning queue.
  • Ski passOnline, at least two days early, for 5% off.
  • Airport transfer or hire carWith winter tyres, or chains in the boot.
  • Hut lunches and dinnersThe popular ones need a reservation in high season.
What to book in advance

Pack

  • HelmetCompulsory, or rent one with your skis.
  • Goggles and sunglassesFlat light one day, glare the next.
  • Sun cream SPF 50 and lip balmThe sun is strong at 2,000 m and above.
  • LayersMornings can be −15 °C, March afternoons feel like spring.
  • ID for every family memberNeeded for child, junior and senior pass prices.
  • Insurance documentsIncluding third-party liability cover.
