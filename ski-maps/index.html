Val Gardena · Alpe di Siusi

The heart of the Sella Ronda on one side, Europe’s largest high alpine meadow on the other. Gentle Alpe di Siusi suits families and beginners; Ciampinoi and the Saslong add steep classics.

Alta Badia

Wide, well-groomed cruising runs between Corvara, Colfosco, La Villa and San Cassiano, with the Gran Risa World Cup slope and some of the best hut food in the Alps.

Arabba · Marmolada

Steeper runs above Arabba at Porta Vescovo, and the Marmolada glacier, the highest skiing in the Dolomites, reached from Malga Ciapela.

Val di Fassa · Carezza

Canazei, Campitello and the Belvedere and Col Rodella lifts link into the Sella Ronda; Carezza, below the Latemar and Rosengarten, is quiet and family-friendly.

Cortina d’Ampezzo

Olympic Cortina: the Tofane, Cinque Torri with Lagazuoi, and Faloria-Cristallo, framed by some of the most spectacular rock in the range.

Kronplatz · Plan de Corones

One rounded summit with lifts and runs on every side, plus a large snowpark. Easy to navigate and quick to ski from any of the base villages.

Tognola, Ces and Colverde above San Martino di Castrozza, and the open slopes of Passo Rolle below the Pale di San Martino.

Alpe Lusia above Moena, and Passo San Pellegrino linked with Falcade: sunny, uncrowded skiing at the southern edge of the Sella group.

Civetta

Alleghe, Zoldo and Selva di Cadore linked below the north face of the Civetta, one of the great walls of the Dolomites.

Bressanone’s own mountain: wide open runs with views to the Odle, and one of the longest toboggan runs in the region. Part of the Rio Pusteria–Bressanone area.
