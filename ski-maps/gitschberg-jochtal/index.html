Two family-run mountains above South Tyrol's oldest town: the Plose, with one of the region's longest descents, and Gitschberg Jochtal, a sunny double peak built for families.

Most Dolomites skiers rush past Bressanone (Brixen) on the Brenner motorway. They miss a lot. The town, South Tyrol's oldest, has been a bishops' seat since the 10th century and has a station on the Brenner railway line. Its home mountain, the Plose, rises just east of the centre, with views south to the Dolomites and north to the main Alpine chain.

At the mouth of the Pusteria valley (Pustertal), Gitschberg Jochtal links the villages of Maranza (Meransen) and Valles (Vals) high above Rio di Pusteria (Mühlbach). Dolomiti Superski groups both as the area Rio Pusteria – Bressanone.

The Double Peak

Gitschberg Jochtal

Two separate ski areas grew up here: Gitschberg above Maranza, where a cable car from Rio di Pusteria opened in 1957, and Jochtal above Valles, which opened in 1976. In 2011 the companies merged and a new cable car joined the two mountains, creating the largest ski area in the Valle Isarco (Eisacktal). Both villages sit at about 1,400 m.

Today there are 55 km of pistes and 15 lifts between 1,307 m and 2,510 m, with 11 blue, 10 red and 4 black pistes. Gondolas climb from both villages, and the 2018 Ski Express, 1,853 m long with 510 m of vertical, carries 3,000 people an hour. The Gaisjoch cable car spans 538 m between supports and hangs as much as 93 m above the ground. Three viewing platforms look out over more than 500 peaks.

Families

Built for Children

Gitschberg Jochtal markets itself first to families, with three ski schools certified as child-friendly and three children's parks with all-day care. The Gimmy Snowpark (2025) has two playgrounds with slides, climbing ropes and snow hills, and the Gimmy Fun Ride funslope has steep curves, small jumps and tunnels. The Skimovie track is a 250 m timed slalom with video, and there are Kids Cross and Speed Check lines on Jochtal (see skiing with kids).

Sledgers get the Brimi Winter Run, a 6.1 km toboggan run with snowmaking from the Gitschberg top station to the valley; see our guide to beyond skiing. South Tyrol's guest cards include regional trains and ski buses (see getting around).

Who It Suits

Who Should Come

The Plose suits strong intermediates and experts who want long, steep runs within sight of a historic town, and travellers arriving by train. Gitschberg Jochtal is ideal for families and relaxed intermediates. Neither links by piste to the Sella Ronda, so they are best as a base for a quieter week or as day trips on the Dolomiti Superski pass (prices in ski passes and prices).

Local tip

Making the most of it

  • Come by train: Bressanone is on the Brenner line (see getting there); local buses are covered in our guide to getting around.
  • Ski the Trametsch first thing, then spend the afternoon on the sunny upper slopes.
  • With small children, base yourself in Maranza or Valles and use the Gimmy parks.

In short

Highlights

  • Sunny, gentle slopes
