At the southern tip of Dolomiti Superski, a village of old hotels sits beneath the pale towers of the Pale di San Martino, with sunny, uncrowded pistes on the slopes opposite.

Few ski villages have a backdrop like San Martino di Castrozza. Above its rooftops the walls of the Pale di San Martino turn rose at sunset, crowned by Cimon della Pala and Vezzana, the group's highest peaks. The skiing lies mostly on the gentler slopes opposite, where gondolas climb through larch and spruce to Alpe Tognola and Punta Ces.

Add the open bowls of Passo Rolle, 9 km up the road, and you have 60 km of pistes far from the Sella Ronda crowds, with a gondola link to the pass now under construction.

The Village

A Resort Below the Pale

San Martino stands at 1,487 m in Trentino's Primiero district. It began as a hospice for travellers crossing the Rolle Pass; only the church, with its Romanesque bell tower, survives. Devastated in the First World War, the village was rebuilt and grew into a resort of grand hotels.

Its position at the southern edge of Dolomiti Superski brings milder temperatures and many sunny days. A ski bus links the village with all lift bases, including Passo Rolle and the beginner slope at Passo Cereda, served by a single drag lift.

The Main Area

Carosello delle Malghe

Most of the skiing is on the Carosello delle Malghe, 45 km of linked pistes between Alpe Tognola and Punta Ces. The Tognola and Colverde gondolas rise from the village, and the Punta Ces four-seat chair has plexiglass bubbles against the wind. Snowmaking covers 95% of the area, and 15 snow-cats groom the runs nightly.

The Valcigolera 10-seat gondola, running since December 2024, climbs 465 m to Cima Tognola (2,383 m) in about three and a half minutes; the old chair took 12. With the Cigolera and Cima Tognola lifts also renewed, Tognola to Ces now takes 6 minutes instead of 18.

Tognola Uno, the Direttissima and Cristiania are the demanding names. The Col Verde piste drops straight to the village and at Christmas is floodlit after sunset. Famous runs are detailed in our guide to famous runs.

Sunny, quiet and ringed by some of the Dolomites' most dramatic rock, San Martino is the Superski's southern balcony.

What'S New

Winter 2026/27 at Alpe Tognola

The big change is at Scandola. A new detachable six-seat chair, 1,324 m long with a 308 m rise and 2,650 people an hour, replaces the Conca and Scandola chairs, cutting stations from four to two and pylons from 33 to 10. A new slope of over 700 m links the Conca and Scandola pistes, and the short Larici run joins Cima Tognola to Scandola.

The San Martino Snowpark is being moved and rebuilt as a freestyle hub with a new Dolomia Funslope (see snowboarding and snowparks), and a new Scandola ski bar gets a large sun terrace.

The Pass

Passo Rolle

At 1,989 m, Passo Rolle links the Primiero and Fiemme valleys, about 20 minutes by ski bus from the village. Its separate area of about 15 km and five lifts, roughly 1,900–2,250 m, faces Cimon della Pala; three lifts climb the northern slope of the Cavallazza. The black Paradiso I and the red Fiamme Gialle are the best-known runs.

The pass's freestyle park lies near the Capanna Cervino hut, netted and signed by difficulty, with rails, boxes, a wall ride and jumps from 2 m to 12 m, plus a boardercross and a snowboard school camp. It was listed as closed in 2025/26; see our guide to snowboarding and snowparks.

Tip Helmets are compulsory in the park, as everywhere on Italian pistes (see safety and slope rules).

The Future

The Gondola to the Pass

In July 2026 the Province of Trento presented the construction site of a road-free link: two integrated 10-seat gondolas forming one 4.6 km system, with stations at Bellaria, Prà delle Nasse, Fosse di Sopra and Passo Rolle, plus a downhill-only stop at Sass Maor. Bellaria to the pass will take about 13 minutes without changing cabins. The project costs about €55 million and is due to open in 2029.

Who It Suits

Who Should Come

San Martino suits intermediates and families who prefer short queues and sunny terraces to mileage. Experts get a handful of steep reds and blacks, beginners the Cereda slope and Rolle's easy runs. The area has its own pass and is on the Dolomiti Superski pass; prices are in our guide to ski passes and prices.

A compact resort of hotels and shops with lifts on its doorstep and the Pale filling the skyline. Base yourself here for the Carosello and take the ski bus to Rolle or Cereda (where to stay: our guide to where to stay).

Tip Watch the Pale turn pink at dusk from the Col Verde run.

Local tip

Making the most of San Martino

  • Ride Valcigolera early to reach the highest runs before the sun softens them.
  • Ski the Carosello as a loop: Tognola to Ces now takes minutes.
  • Go up to Rolle on a clear day for the classic view of Cimon della Pala.
