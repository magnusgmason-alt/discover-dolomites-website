Val di Fiemme · Obereggen

Obereggen, Pampeago, Predazzo and Cermis

Two provinces, one ski pass: the Latemar's linked slopes between Obereggen, Pampeago and Predazzo, the long runs of Alpe Cermis and a valley steeped in Nordic skiing and Olympic history.

Val di Fiemme is the valley of the Avisio river in Trentino, with Cavalese as its administrative centre and Predazzo its economic one, and Tesero and Ziano di Fiemme in between. On the far side of the Latemar massif, Obereggen looks down the Eggental (Val d'Ega) towards Bolzano in South Tyrol.

One Dolomiti Superski area, Val di Fiemme/Obereggen, ties them together. Its pass covers all the lifts in the Fiemme valley and in Obereggen: 120 km of slopes by Dolomiti Superski's count (the area's own piste map shows 111 km).

The Main Event

Ski Center Latemar

The core of the area is Ski Center Latemar, which links Obereggen in South Tyrol with Pampeago and Predazzo in Trentino, so you can ski between the provinces without taking your skis off. It counts 48 km of slopes, 18 lifts, 12 ski huts, three ski kindergartens and a snowpark, with some 300 snow cannons behind it.

New this winter, an 8-seat Leitner chairlift replaces the 2006 four-seater on the key link between Obereggen and the Pampeago–Predazzo side. It is 500 m long with a 93 m rise, carries up to 2,800 people an hour and takes under two minutes. A new Eben drag lift and a redesigned Brunoland kids' park, now beside the Obereggen valley station, open with it (see skiing with kids).

Obereggen is the Latemar's place for evenings on snow. The Ochsenweide 8-seat gondola serves a floodlit slope of about 1.8 km, a 2.5 km toboggan run with a new forest finish near the Oberholz chairlift, and a 1,780 m Nightpark. Multi-day, weekly and season passes include evening lifts.

Tip Students get half-price evening passes on Tuesdays (Uni Night) with a student card.

The Trentino side climbs from Pampeago and Predazzo to the Latemar's southern slopes. At Pampeago the Agnello run passes the open-air artworks of RespirArt, while Pala di Santa starts at 2,400 m. A new water reservoir at Pampeago boosts snowmaking this winter. Predazzo is also the town for the area's ski-jumping history (see below).

The Latemar Sixpack

Obereggen markets its six steepest runs as the Latemar Sixpack. Here they are in brief; our guide to famous runs has the detail. For lighter fun, the nine Latemar Fun Lines add banked turns, rollers and tunnels, plus timed runs and an automatic Skimovie camera.

Across The Valley

Cermis, Bellamonte and Oclini

Alpe Cermis is known for the long Olimpia run and the red Busabella. Its slopes are also a stage of cross-country skiing's Tour de Ski, which has ended here with the brutal Final Climb since 2007. Renovation and modernisation work is under way at Cermis this winter. Run detail: our guide to famous runs.

Bellamonte–Alpe Lusia has runs reaching into the Paneveggio–Pale di San Martino nature park and views of the Lagorai range; its snowmaking was renewed for this winter, and its Morea Snowpark is in our guide to snowboarding and snowparks. The Lusia side is covered in our guide to Alpe Lusia and San Pellegrino. The small Jochgrimm-Oclini and Lavazè plateau areas combine alpine and Nordic skiing.

Nordic And Olympic

A Valley of Champions

Val di Fiemme has hosted the Nordic World Ski Championships three times, in 1991, 2003 and 2013. At the 2026 Winter Olympics, the renovated Predazzo Ski Jumping Stadium staged ski jumping and the jumping part of the Nordic combined, while the rebuilt Tesero Cross-Country Skiing Stadium held cross-country, Nordic combined and Paralympic biathlon and cross-country races.

The Lavazè and Lake Tesero centres offer some 150 km of cross-country trails, including the Marcialonga track (see beyond skiing). Several village extras are covered by the ski pass, including the ice rinks in Cavalese and Tesero and the swimming pool in Predazzo.

Ski between two provinces by day, then take the floodlit run down to Obereggen by night.

Who It Suits

Is Fiemme for You?

The Latemar suits families and intermediates who want a compact, well-linked area with ski kindergartens and evening skiing, and experts who want a handful of genuinely steep runs. Cross-country skiers will find one of the Dolomites' great Nordic valleys. The Valle Silver pass adds Val di Fassa-Carezza, Alpe Lusia–San Pellegrino and San Martino-Passo Rolle (see ski passes and prices).

Local tip
