Civetta

Alleghe, Pescul, Selva di Cadore and Palafavera

Beneath one of the great rock walls of the Alps, three Venetian valleys share a forested, family-sized ski area: lakeside Alleghe, sunny Selva di Cadore and quiet Val di Zoldo.

Above the village of Alleghe and its lake rises Monte Civetta, a 3,220 m massif whose north-west face is a cliff about 1,000 m high, first climbed in 1925 by Emil Solleder and Gustl Lettenbauer. Across the valleys stands the great block of Pelmo, and between the two lies some of the quietest skiing in Dolomiti Superski.

The ski area that takes the mountain's name links three valleys in the Province of Belluno. It lacks the glamour of Cortina, but repays visitors with tree-lined runs, short queues and classic Venetian hospitality.

The Area

One Mountain, Three Valleys

Ski Civetta was created in 1982, when the lifts of Selva di Cadore, running since 1978, were joined with those of Alleghe and Val di Zoldo. The area entered Dolomiti Superski in winter 1993/94 and today promotes itself as Veneto's largest ski area, with more than 80 km of pistes and 23 lifts; Dolomiti Superski's live list counts 47 runs, only four of them black. Unlike the Sella Ronda areas, it has no single central village: you choose a valley, then ski across into the others.

Most of the skiing lies at or below the tree line, between about 1,000 m at the lake village of Alleghe and about 2,100 m on Monte Fertazza, the area's highest point. Forest gives shelter, which makes Civetta a good choice on grey, windy days, when runs among the trees keep their definition while open bowls elsewhere turn flat and featureless.

Alleghe sits on the shore of a lake created in 1771, when a landslide from Monte Piz dammed the valley. A gondola climbs from the village into the ski area. In winter the frozen lake draws skaters, and the village is a hockey town, with its own stadium, the Stadio del ghiaccio Alvise De Toni.

Tip Ride the gondola down at day's end if the lower slopes are icy.

Above the village, the Alleghe Boardercross, served by the Pelmo lift, is one of the area's three cross and park lines (see snowboarding and snowparks).

Did you know?

A wall with a history

Monte Civetta may first have been climbed in 1855 by Simeone de Silvestro, though the ascent is unconfirmed. The north-west face waited another 70 years: Emil Solleder and Gustl Lettenbauer reached the top by that 1,000 m wall in 1925. Alleghe has a sporting streak of its own: its ice hockey club, HC Alleghe, were surprise winners of the 1992/93 Alpine League.

Val Fiorentina

Selva di Cadore and Pescul

North of Alleghe, Selva di Cadore (1,350 m) and its hamlet Pescul lie in the Val Fiorentina, about 120 km north of Venice. From Pescul, chairlifts climb to Monte Fertazza, whose summit looks out to Pelmo and the surrounding Dolomites.

Several of Civetta's most enjoyable runs start here. Salere is a red of about 3.5 km with a 700 m drop, a satisfying, sustained descent. The short red Fertazza suits a warm-up, the blue Le Ciaune is gentle enough for beginners, and the red Bait is a technical line through the woods down to Pescul. More on the region's classic descents is in our guide to famous runs.

Civetta trades glamour for forest, space and the evening glow on Pelmo and Civetta.

The Quiet Valley

Val di Zoldo

On the eastern side, Val di Zoldo connects through the hamlets of Pecol and the Palafavera pass. The Delle Coste four-seat chair, opened in 2023, links Val di Zoldo with Palafavera and on towards Alleghe and Selva di Cadore; it is 873 m long, rises 210 m and carries 2,000 people an hour, against 1,200 on the old two-seater.

From Pecol, a gondola climbs to the restaurant at Pian del Crep, and the Boardercross Val di Zoldo runs on the Casot di Pecol–Col de la Grava lift. Beginners and young riders also have the Mini Snowpark Mariaz on the Mariaz school slope.

Who It Suits

Who Should Come

Civetta is at its best for beginners, intermediates and families who value long, quiet forest runs and good-value valley accommodation over big-name mileage. Experts get a few long reds rather than a week of steeps. Groups who want variety can base themselves here on the Dolomiti Superski pass and drive to Cortina or Arabba for a day; pass prices are in our guide to ski passes and prices, driving and roads in our guide to getting around.

Local tip

Civetta tips

  • On a stormy day, head for the forest runs above Pescul and Val di Zoldo.
  • On a clear day, ride to Monte Fertazza for the view of Pelmo and Civetta.
  • Hut culture here is Venetian: see our guide to mountain huts and food for dishes such as casunziei.
