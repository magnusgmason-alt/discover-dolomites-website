Val di Fassa · Carezza

Canazei, Campitello, Belvedere, Col Rodella and Carezza

Trentino's Ladin valley runs from the Sella and Pordoi passes down past the Catinaccio, with lively Canazei on the Sella Ronda and, over the Costalunga pass, the quieter slopes of Carezza beneath the Latemar.

Val di Fassa is the Ladin valley of Trentino, about 20 km long from Moena up to Canazei, with the Sella, the Sassolungo and the Marmolada at its head and the Catinaccio (Rosengarten) along its western flank. Skiing here began early: Canazei's first ski lift opened in 1947.

The valley's ski areas are separate islands linked by lifts, ski tours and buses: Belvedere–Col Rodella on the Sella Ronda, Buffaure–Ciampac, Catinaccio above Vigo and, over the Costalunga pass, Carezza. One Dolomiti Superski area, Val di Fassa/Carezza, covers them all.

The Area

Four Ski Areas, One Pass

Val di Fassa does not form one seamless circuit like Alta Badia; it is a chain of distinct areas, each with its own character. The valley's tourist board publishes figures for each, and the four that make up the Val di Fassa/Carezza Superski area add up to about 110 km of slopes and 54 lifts. Alpe Lusia above Moena and San Pellegrino belong to a separate Superski area (see Alpe Lusia and San Pellegrino).

On The Sella Ronda

Canazei and Campitello

Belvedere–Col Rodella is the valley's big, busy area, reached from Canazei on one side and Campitello di Fassa on the other. The two flanks run almost parallel and meet at Pian Frataces (Lupo Bianco) on the road to the Sella and Pordoi passes. The runs are mainly wide reds with long descents, better for confident intermediates than first-timers; the black Diego on Belvedere is the technical test, and the 3-Tre follows Val Salei for more than 3 km from Col Rodella.

The valley's liveliest resort and its Sella Ronda base, with Italy's oldest ski school, according to the regional portal. From the centre a gondola climbs to Pecol, and a cable car continues to Col dei Rossi on Belvedere. A ski bus links Canazei and Campitello, so you can ski down one side and ride back.

Tip The Ski FunArena on Belvedere has banked turns and a funslope for children and teens.

A smaller village just down the valley from Canazei. Its tri-cable car rises to Col Rodella, an access route onto the Sella Ronda towards Passo Sella and Val Gardena (see Val Gardena).

From Passo Pordoi, on the Sella Ronda between Canazei and Arabba (see Arabba and the Marmolada), a cable car lifts you onto the flat summit of the Sella's southern buttress. For 2026/27 fassa.com lists a later winter start than the ski area, from 22 December (the summer season ends on 1 November), so check before you go. The view across to the Marmolada is the reward; off-piste descents from the top are for experts with a guide only (see freeride and avalanche safety).

Mid-Valley

Buffaure, Ciampac and Catinaccio

Buffaure–Ciampac links Pozza di Fassa with Alba di Canazei over the Sela Brunéch (2,428 m). Its showpiece runs are the red Panorama, 5.1 km to Pozza, the Vulcano black with more than 500 m of drop, and the Ciampac black to Alba, over 2 km and about 660 m through the woods. In Pozza the Aloch stadium is a training slope for national teams and hosts a men's European Cup slalom each December.

Fassa's main new lift this winter replaces the old chairlift up to Col Valvacin, part of Dolomiti Superski's investment of about €100 million for 2026/27. It sits on the route from Pozza via Buffaure and Val Jumela to Ciampac. Buffaure also has a snowpark with a Big Air Bag (see snowboarding and snowparks).

Above Vigo di Fassa, Catinaccio is small and scenic, facing the Catinaccio group and the Vajolet Towers. A 101-person cable car climbs from Vigo to Ciampedie, at about 2,000 m; from Pra Martin the red Thöni runs about 4.5 km back to Vigo, and the short Alberto Tomba black tests the confident. The Kinderpark at Ciampedie suits small children. A new gondola from Pera to Pozza is planned to link Catinaccio with Buffaure.

Over The Pass

Carezza

Passo Costalunga, Trentino–South Tyrol

Carezza

Villages
Nova Levante (Welschnofen), Carezza, Tires (Tiers)
Ski tour
Rosengarten Ronda

Beneath the Latemar and the Catinaccio, Carezza is reached over Passo Costalunga on the Great Dolomites Road, built in 1909. Ten-seat gondolas rise from Nova Levante to Malga Frommer and up the König Laurin sector; Pra di Tori has been a Snowboard World Cup stage, and the Carezza Snowpark draws freestylers (see snowboarding and snowparks). Ski buses connect with Vigo, Pozza, Obereggen (see Val di Fiemme and Obereggen) and the Alpe di Siusi.

Tip Stop at Lake Carezza, beside the road below the pass, and stay for the enrosadira glow on the peaks at sunset.

Who It Suits

Choosing Your Corner

  • Sella Ronda skiers and groups: Canazei or Campitello, for Belvedere–Col Rodella and the ring.
  • Intermediates who like long runs: Pozza, for Buffaure–Ciampac and the Panorama tour.
  • Families: Vigo for Catinaccio's Kinderpark, or Carezza's quieter slopes.
  • Freestylers: Buffaure and Carezza, with the valley's snowparks.

Local tip

  • The Panorama ski tour links Catinaccio, Buffaure, Ciampac and Belvedere; ski it to see the whole valley in a day.
  • Belvedere–Col Rodella is the valley's liveliest and busiest area; aim for an early start.
  • Valley ski buses join the areas and the Canazei–Campitello sides; details in our guide to getting around.

In short

Highlights

  • The Ciampac and Buffaure areas above Alba and Pozza
