The Dolomites

One mountain range. Two seasons.

In summer the Dolomites are green alpine meadows, wildflowers and trails that lead from hut to hut beneath towers of pale rock. In winter the same valleys vanish under snow, and the passes become one of the largest ski areas in the world. Two seasons, two completely different mountains, and both are worth the journey.

Winter. Snow on pale stone.

The pale mountains

Built from coral. Lit like fire.

Long before the Alps rose, the Dolomites were a warm tropical sea. Their peaks are the remains of reefs and atolls more than 200 million years old. At dawn and dusk the pale rock glows pink and orange, a display the Ladin people call enrosadira.

UNESCO

World Heritage

The Dolomites, inscribed in 2009

Nine mountain groups and 141,903 hectares of pale peaks are protected as a single World Heritage Site, recognised for their beauty and for what their rock reveals about how mountains form and about life in the warm seas of the Triassic.
