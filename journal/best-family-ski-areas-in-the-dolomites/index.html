The Best Family Ski Areas in the Dolomites

5 min read · Updated 27 October 2026

The Dolomites are one of the best places in the Alps to ski as a family. The slopes are wide and sunny, the mountain huts serve proper lunches, and many areas have built dedicated kids' zones with magic carpets, gentle runs and playgrounds in the snow. But the ski areas are not all equal for children. Here are the best family ski areas in the Dolomites, and what makes each one work.

What makes a ski area good for families?

  • Gentle, wide blue runs close to the lifts, so children can build confidence without long traverses.
  • Magic carpets and beginner lifts that small children can use on their own.
  • A ski school with a kids' area or ski kindergarten, so parents can ski while children learn.
  • Things to do off the slopes: sledging, skating, playgrounds and easy winter walks for rest days.
  • Short, simple connections, so the whole family can ski together without getting stuck on a steep red run.

1. Alpe di Siusi (Seiser Alm)

If there is one classic family choice, it is Alpe di Siusi. The high plateau shares a ski area with Val Gardena of around 181 km of slopes and 79 lifts, and the slopes up on the alm are wide, sunny and gentle. There are also around 80 km of cross-country trails for parents who prefer to glide.

For children there is a village lift in Castelrotto for first tries, the Marinzen Alm family area with a conveyor-belt sledging slope, an ice rink and a playground, and kids' fun parks along the Seiser Alm Ronda circuit. The area has a ski kindergarten and two ski schools. Even better, the plateau is closed to private cars during the day, so the whole place feels calm and safe. The 2026/27 season runs from 4 December to 4 April.

2. Carezza

Carezza is small, and that is exactly its strength. Around 40 km of slopes sit together on one plateau below the Rosengarten (Catinaccio), so families never get lost or split up. The lower part of the area is made up of blue beginner runs, served by a magic carpet and lifts with children's seats.

There are three kids' parks, including König Laurin Kinderland, which combines a ski school with childcare. And Carezza has one of the best deals for young families: children up to 8 years old ski free when an adult pass is bought at the same time.

3. Obereggen

Obereggen, in the Latemar area, is compact (around 48 km of slopes) and well organised for children. Its Yeti Park is a supervised kids' area for ages 4 and up, with a magic carpet and slopes of different steepness, run by ski instructors.

Older children and teenagers will love the Obereggen snowpark, which includes a Family Fun Line and a Kids Learn Park alongside the bigger jumps. It is one of the few areas in Italy with a halfpipe, and the park is even open at night three times a week.

4. Alta Badia

Alta Badia is a great choice for families with mixed abilities, because it combines easy slopes and beginner areas with access to the much larger Sella Ronda network for confident parents. The valley has kids' fun parks in Colfosco and San Cassiano (Movimënt Kidslope at Pralongià), and six ski and snowboard schools across its villages.

For complete beginners aged 3 to 5, Alta Badia runs a dedicated KIDS ON SKI package: seven nights with five half-day lessons, five days of ski passes and five days of equipment rental. It is a stress-free way to get very young children started.

5. Val Gardena

Val Gardena is lively, well connected and full of options for children. Monte Pana above Santa Cristina has a kids' fun park and practice lifts, Ortisei has a children's slope at Furdenan, and the snowpark at Piz Sella below Sassolungo is designed for beginners and families. Because Val Gardena connects to Alpe di Siusi, you can also mix the two areas.

6. Plan de Corones (Kronplatz)

Plan de Corones is a big, modern ski area, best for families with children who already ski. It has a dedicated kids' world at Valdaora, sledge runs for small children and snowtubing, and its snowpark has a Family Line for parents and kids to ride together.

Which one should you choose?

For toddlers and first-timers: Carezza or Alpe di Siusi. For children aged 4 to 8 who want to learn properly: Obereggen or Alta Badia. For families with older children and confident parents: Val Gardena, Alta Badia or Plan de Corones, with the whole Dolomiti Superski network to explore.

Tips for a family ski week in the Dolomites

  • Book ski school early if you are travelling at Christmas, New Year or in February, when kids' groups fill up.
  • Helmets are compulsory for everyone on Italian slopes, adults and children alike. Read our guide to Dolomites ski rules and safety.
  • Plan a rest day. Sledging, ice skating and horse-drawn sleigh rides are a highlight for most children.
  • Stay close to the lifts. Carrying skis and small children through a village every morning gets old quickly.
  • Lunch at a mountain hut is part of the experience. Booking a table on busy days avoids a long wait.
