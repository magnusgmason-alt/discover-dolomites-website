Ski Schools for Kids in the Dolomites: Ages, Courses and Booking

5 min read · Updated 27 October 2026

Ski school is the heart of most family ski holidays in the Dolomites. Children learn faster with an instructor and other kids their age, and parents get a few hours on the slopes. Every main village has at least one ski and snowboard school, and many have their own fenced kids' area. Here is how they work, from the right age to start to booking, prices and what to pack.

What age can children start?

  • Age 3 to 4: ski kindergartens and "first steps" programmes, with play-based lessons in a fenced snow garden with magic carpets.
  • Age 4 to 5 and up: group ski courses. Many schools take children from 4, grouped by age and level.
  • Age 6 to 8 and up: snowboard lessons usually start here, depending on the school.
  • Under 3: very few schools teach this young. Private lessons or simply playing in the snow are a better start.

How group courses work

The classic format is a course over five or six days, usually Monday or Sunday to Friday. Groups are small (often 4 to 8 children) and sorted by age and ability on the first morning. Most schools offer:

  • Half-day courses, typically two to three hours in the morning.
  • Full-day courses with supervised lunch, so parents can ski all day.
  • An end-of-week race or show, often with medals, which children love.

As one example, Ski School 2000 in Selva Gardena runs kids' group lessons for ages 4 to 12 in groups of 4 to 7, meeting at its Kinderland, with a full-day excursion and hut lunch once a week.

Book early

Over Christmas, New Year and the February school holidays, kids' groups fill weeks in advance. Book lessons as soon as you have booked your accommodation.

Private lessons

Private lessons are worth it for very young or nervous children, for one or two sessions before joining a group, or for siblings at the same level who can share an instructor. Expect prices from about €60 to €110 per hour, with lower hourly rates for half or full days. Group courses are much cheaper per hour, and children usually enjoy them more.

Where to find good kids' areas

  • Alpe di Siusi: gentle, sunny slopes, ski kindergartens and a kids' club at Compatsch.
  • Val Gardena: several schools with Kinderlands in Selva, Santa Cristina and Ortisei.
  • Alta Badia: six ski schools and programmes for complete beginners aged 3 to 5.
  • Val di Fassa: kids' areas such as Kinderland near the Col Rodella cable car, from age 3.
  • Obereggen and Carezza: compact, easy to manage, with supervised kids' parks.

We compare these areas in more detail in the best family ski areas in the Dolomites.

Language

Instructors in South Tyrol usually teach in German, Italian and English, and in Ladin valleys many also speak Ladin. Ask for an English-speaking group when you book. Children often end up in mixed-language groups and cope fine, since much of the teaching is by showing.

What children need

  • A ski pass from the first day for most courses, except in some beginner areas. See what children pay for ski passes.
  • A helmet, compulsory for all skiers in Italy. Rent one with the skis if needed.
  • Waterproof gloves and spares, goggles, sun cream and a snack in the pocket.
  • A card with your phone number in their jacket.

Tips for the first day

  • Arrive early. The first morning is busy with sorting groups and finding equipment.
  • Collect rental gear the afternoon before, so the morning is calm.
  • Tell the instructor the truth about your child's level. A group that is too hard ruins the week.
  • Stay out of sight for the first hour. Children settle faster without parents watching.
