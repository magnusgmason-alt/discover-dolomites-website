Skiing in the Dolomites with Kids: The Complete Family Guide

5 min read · Updated 27 October 2026

Skiing in the Dolomites with kids is easier, and better value, than many parents expect. The slopes are wide and sunny, ski schools have dedicated kids' areas, and young children can ski for free. This guide covers everything you need to plan a family ski trip: which areas to choose, what children pay, ski school ages, the rules on helmets and insurance, and how to keep everyone happy for a week.

What age can children start skiing?

Most children in the Dolomites start skiing at three or four years old. Ski kindergartens such as Kinderland near the Col Rodella cable car in Val di Fassa take children from age 3, and Alta Badia's KIDS ON SKI package is designed for complete beginners aged 3 to 5. Group lessons for children typically run from around 4 years old. Younger children are usually happiest in the snow play areas, on a sledge or in a kids' park.

Kids ski free: what children pay

The Dolomiti Superski pricing for winter 2026/27 is very family-friendly:

  • Children born after 1 January 2019 ski free when an adult buys a pass. It is one free child per paying adult, and the child's pass must be the same type and duration as the adult's.
  • Juniors born between 2009 and 2018 get 30% off, which makes a high-season day pass €62 instead of €89.
  • Large families: a third junior child can get a free multi-day pass when bought together with two adult passes, with proof of family status.

The free-child offer does not apply to some tickets, such as afternoon tickets and a few special products, so check before you buy. Several valleys also run their own family weeks: Val Gardena, for example, has a spring family offer with free passes for children up to 8.

The best areas for skiing with kids

For toddlers and first-timers, Carezza and Alpe di Siusi are hard to beat: gentle slopes, magic carpets and dedicated family zones. For children who want to learn properly, Obereggen (with its supervised Yeti Park for ages 4 and up) and Alta Badia are excellent. Families with older children and confident parents will love Val Gardena and Plan de Corones, which has a big kids' area at Valdaora with three magic carpets, tubing and a skating rink. We compare them in detail in the best family ski areas in the Dolomites.

Ski school: how it works

Every main village has at least one ski and snowboard school, and many have their own kids' area. Children usually join a group course over five or six mornings, grouped by age and ability, with a small race or show at the end of the week. Some schools offer full-day options with lunch, which gives parents a proper day on the slopes.

Book early

Over Christmas, New Year and the February school holidays, children's groups fill up fast. Book lessons when you book your accommodation.

Helmets and insurance: the rules for families

Italy has its own ski law, and two rules matter for every family:

  • Helmets are compulsory for everyone. Since November 2025 the rule covers skiers and snowboarders of all ages, not just children, and it also applies to sledging. You can be fined, and repeat offenders risk losing their ski pass.
  • Third-party liability insurance is compulsory. Every skier must be insured for damage they cause to others. It is not included in the ski pass, but you can buy day cover when you buy your passes, or check whether your travel insurance already includes it.

Read the full details in our guide to Dolomites ski rules, helmets and insurance.

Planning the day around children

  • Start late, finish early. Children ski best in the late morning and early afternoon, when it is warmer.
  • Lunch at a mountain hut is the highlight of the day for many kids. Book a table on busy days.
  • Stay near the lifts or the ski school. Short walks in ski boots make mornings far easier.
  • Plan a rest day for sledging, ice skating or a horse-drawn sleigh ride.
  • Pack spares: extra gloves and socks, sun cream and goggles. The sun at altitude is strong, even in January.

Safety on the slopes with children

Teach children to stop at the side of the piste, never in the middle or just below a ridge, and to look uphill before setting off. Agree a meeting point in case you are separated, and put a card with your phone number in their jacket. In an emergency, call 112. Ski patrols and lift staff are always the first people to ask for help.

When to go skiing with kids

January, after the New Year holidays, and March are usually the best months for families: good snow, fewer crowds and more space in ski school. The 2026/27 Dolomiti Superski season runs from 28 November to early May, with most areas open from early December to early April.
